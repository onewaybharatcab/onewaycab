Delhi to Jaipur by Cab: The Complete 2026 Travel Guide

The 281 km Delhi–Jaipur highway is one of India's most beautiful intercity drives. Here's the best pit stops, when to travel, what to expect on the road, and why a one-way cab beats a bus or train for this route.

Route & Travel Guides

The drive from Delhi to Jaipur is often called the gateway leg of the Golden Triangle, and it earns the reputation — NH48 is a smooth, well-signed four-to-six lane expressway for almost the entire 281 km, and the pink-sandstone skyline of Rajasthan's capital appears with barely a bad stretch of road in between. Here's what actually matters for planning the trip: the route, the timing, where to stop, and how a one-way cab compares to the alternatives.

The Route: What NH48 Actually Looks Like

NH48 runs almost dead straight from Delhi through Gurugram, Manesar, Behror, Shahpura and Chandwaji before reaching Jaipur — a mix of the Delhi–Jaipur Expressway and older NH8 stretches, most of it now widened to six lanes. The road surface is consistently good, tolls are collected at a handful of plazas along the way, and unless you hit Gurugram at rush hour, there's very little urban congestion to worry about.

Expect roughly 5–6 hours of driving time depending on traffic through Gurugram and Manesar in the first hour, which is where almost all of the route's delay tends to happen. Once you clear Manesar, the road opens up and the rest of the drive moves quickly.

Best Time to Travel

Early morning departures (6–7 AM) avoid both Gurugram's peak-hour traffic and Rajasthan's midday heat if you're travelling between April and June. October to March is the most comfortable window overall — cool mornings, clear skies, and no risk of the summer heat haze that can make the last hour into Jaipur feel long.

If you can, avoid starting a Delhi–Jaipur drive after 4 PM on a Friday — this corridor is a popular weekend getaway route and traffic through Gurugram can back up significantly.

Where to Stop Along the Way

Most travellers break the journey at one of the highway plazas around Neemrana or Shahpura, roughly the halfway point, for tea, breakfast or a quick meal. Neemrana itself is a worthwhile short detour if you have extra time — it's home to a restored 15th-century hill fort turned heritage hotel, about 15 minutes off the highway.

If you're travelling with kids or prefer a sit-down meal, the well-known highway restaurant chains around Behror and Shahpura are reliable, clean options with parking right off NH48.

Cab vs Train vs Bus: What Actually Makes Sense

The Shatabdi Express covers Delhi to Jaipur in about 4.5 hours and is a fine option if you're travelling solo with light luggage and don't mind a fixed departure time from a specific station. Buses are the cheapest option but add real time — figure 6–7 hours door to door once you account for the bus stand at both ends.

A one-way cab sits in between on cost but wins clearly on convenience: door-to-door pickup, no fixed schedule, room for luggage, and — if you're travelling as a family or group of 3-4 — it often works out cheaper per person than train tickets, with none of the station-transfer hassle.

💡 Quick Tips

Good to Know Before You Go

⛽
Fuel up before Gurugram if you're driving yourself — stations thin out for a stretch after Manesar.
🎫
If catching the Shatabdi instead, book at least a few days ahead — it sells out quickly on weekends.
🧳
A sedan comfortably fits 3 passengers with luggage; for 4+ or extra bags, an Ertiga or Innova is the better call.
📵
Mobile network is reliable for almost the entire route — a rare, welcome thing on an Indian highway drive.
❓ FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

How long does the Delhi to Jaipur drive take?
+

Around 5–6 hours by cab via NH48, depending on traffic through Gurugram in the first hour of the journey.

Is NH48 safe to drive on at night?
+

The highway is well-lit and well-patrolled for most of its length, though as with any Indian highway, a daylight departure is generally more comfortable, especially for a first-time drive.

What's the cheapest way to get from Delhi to Jaipur?
+

Buses are typically the cheapest per-seat option, though a one-way cab often costs less per person than train tickets once you're travelling as a group of 3 or more.
