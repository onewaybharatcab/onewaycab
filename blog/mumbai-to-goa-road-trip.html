Mumbai to Goa Road Trip: Route, Stops & Cab Tips

590 km of coastal highway magic. We cover the NH66 coastal route, the best places to stop, and how to split cab costs for a group.

Route & Travel Guides

The Mumbai–Goa drive along NH66 is one of India's classic coastal road trips — the Konkan coastline, a string of laid-back beach towns, and a highway that's been steadily upgraded over the past several years to make what used to be a grueling 14-hour haul into a much more manageable long day (or an easy two-day trip if you break it up).

The NH66 Coastal Route

NH66 hugs the Konkan coast almost the entire way from Mumbai down through Raigad, Ratnagiri and Sindhudurg districts before crossing into Goa. Large sections have been widened to four lanes in recent years, though you'll still hit the occasional ghat (hill) section with tighter curves, particularly around the Kashedi and Parshuram ghats.

Total drive time runs 11–12 hours non-stop, which is why most travellers either split it into two days with an overnight stop in the Konkan, or do it as a genuinely long single-day drive with an early start.

Best Places to Stop

Alibaug and Murud-Janjira, about 2–3 hours out of Mumbai, make a good first stop if you're breaking the trip early — Janjira Fort in particular is a striking sea-fort worth the short detour. Further south, Ganpatipule and Ratnagiri (roughly the halfway point) are popular overnight stops with beach access and Konkani seafood.

If you have flexibility, Tarkarli, near the Maharashtra–Goa border, is worth a night for its clear-water beaches and scuba-diving options — a quieter alternative to Goa's busier beaches just a couple of hours before you arrive.

One Day vs Two Days

Doing it in a single day means an early (5–6 AM) start from Mumbai and arriving in Goa after dark — doable, but a long day behind the wheel or in a cab. Splitting it into two days with an overnight in Ratnagiri or Tarkarli turns the drive itself into part of the holiday rather than just a means to an end.

For a group trip, the two-day version is usually worth the extra cost — it roughly halves each day's driving and lets you actually enjoy the Konkan coast rather than just passing through it.

Splitting Cab Costs for a Group

For 4–6 people, an Innova Crysta or Tempo Traveller works out significantly cheaper per person than multiple sedans or flights, especially if you're also planning to use the vehicle for local sightseeing once in Goa.

If you're only going one way and flying back (or vice versa), a one-way cab avoids paying for a driver's return leg — often a meaningfully cheaper option than a round-trip fare for a route this long.

💡 Quick Tips

Good to Know Before You Go

🌅
An early morning start (before 6 AM) gets you clear of Mumbai traffic and into the Konkan ghats before the day heats up.
🦐
Stop for a Konkani seafood thali around Ratnagiri or Devgad — it's one of the highlights of this drive.
🚗
For 4+ travellers, an Innova Crysta or Tempo Traveller is usually cheaper per person than splitting into multiple cars.
🛣️
The ghat sections (Kashedi, Parshuram) have tighter curves — comfortable in daylight, best avoided after dark if you're unfamiliar with the road.
❓ FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

How long does the Mumbai to Goa drive take?
+

About 11–12 hours non-stop by road via NH66; many travellers split it across two days with an overnight stop in the Konkan region.

What's the best route from Mumbai to Goa?
+

NH66, the coastal highway, is the standard route — largely widened to four lanes with a few hill (ghat) sections that require more careful driving.

Is it cheaper to fly or drive from Mumbai to Goa?
+

Flights are usually faster and can be similarly priced for a solo traveller, but for a group of 4 or more, a shared cab often works out cheaper overall, especially if you also need transport once in Goa.
