Haridwar marks the point where the Ganga leaves the Himalayas for the plains, and has been a major Hindu pilgrimage centre for over two thousand years. Its ghats — especially Har Ki Pauri, the site of a spectacular nightly Ganga Aarti — draw pilgrims and travellers year-round, alongside its role as one of four Kumbh Mela host cities.

📜 History & Heritage

The Story of Haridwar

Haridwar's name translates roughly to "Gateway to God" (or, read the other way, "Gateway to Shiva") and it is counted among Hinduism's seven holiest cities (Sapta Puri). Ancient texts including the Puranas reference the site as Gangadwar — the point where the Ganga leaves the Himalayan foothills and reaches the plains, making it sacred to pilgrims for over two millennia.

The city's most iconic ghat, Har Ki Pauri, is believed to mark the spot where drops of amrita, the mythical nectar of immortality, fell to earth during the churning of the cosmic ocean — the same legend that underpins the Kumbh Mela. Haridwar is one of only four cities (with Prayagraj, Nashik and Ujjain) that host this vast pilgrimage gathering, held here roughly every twelve years and drawing millions of devotees.

Beyond its religious status, Haridwar has long served as a practical gateway too — historically a trading town linking the plains to the Himalayan pilgrimage routes further north, and today the nearest major railhead and transit hub for Rishikesh and the wider Garhwal Himalayan region.

🧭 Tourist Information

Top Places to Visit in Haridwar

Har Ki Pauri, Ganga Aarti & Kumbh Mela — here's what most visitors build their itinerary around.

🕯️
Har Ki Pauri
Haridwar's holiest ghat, famous for its nightly Ganga Aarti when thousands of oil lamps are set afloat on the river.
🚡
Mansa Devi Temple
A hilltop temple reachable by cable car, dedicated to a form of the goddess Shakti, with sweeping views over the city.
🚡
Chandi Devi Temple
Another cable-car-accessible hilltop temple across the river, associated with the goddess Chandi from Hindu mythology.
🐘
Rajaji National Park
A tiger and elephant reserve on Haridwar's outskirts, offering jeep safaris through Shivalik forest.
🛍️
Bara Bazaar
Haridwar's old market lanes, known for brassware, Ayurvedic products and traditional sweets.
🌊
Sapt Rishi Ashram Ghat
A quieter, greener stretch of riverfront away from the main ghats, said to mark where the Ganga split into seven streams.

💡 Local Tips

Good to Know Before You Go

🕯️
Reach Har Ki Pauri by 5:30–6 PM to find a good ghat-side spot before the evening Ganga Aarti begins.
🚫
Haridwar is officially vegetarian and alcohol-free within city limits, in keeping with its status as a pilgrimage town.
👗
Modest clothing is appreciated at all ghats and temples, especially for women.
🚗
During Kumbh Mela years and major festivals, roads get extremely congested — book cabs well in advance and expect longer travel times.

Haridwar Travel — Frequently Asked Questions

How far is Haridwar from Delhi by road?
+

Haridwar is about 214 km from Delhi, roughly 4–5 hours by cab via Meerut and Roorkee.

What is Har Ki Pauri famous for?
+

It's Haridwar's holiest ghat, believed to be where drops of the mythical nectar of immortality fell to earth, and it hosts a spectacular nightly Ganga Aarti that's one of the city's biggest draws.

Is Haridwar one of the Kumbh Mela cities?
+

Yes — Haridwar is one of four cities (along with Prayagraj, Nashik and Ujjain) that host the Kumbh Mela on a rotating roughly twelve-year cycle.

Can Haridwar and Rishikesh be visited in one trip?
+

Yes, easily — the two cities are only about 25 km apart and are commonly combined into a single 2–3 day pilgrimage trip.
