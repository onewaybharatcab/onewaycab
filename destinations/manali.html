Manali is Himachal Pradesh's best-known hill town, cradled in the Kullu Valley on the Beas River at around 2,050 m and ringed by pine, deodar forest and snow-capped peaks. It's part adventure base — paragliding, skiing, river rafting — part honeymoon classic, and the last major town before the high Himalayan passes toward Leh and Spiti.

📜 History & Heritage

The Story of Manali

Manali's name is said to derive from "Manu-Alaya" — the abode of Manu, the sage of Hindu tradition believed to have stepped ashore here after a great flood and re-founded human civilisation. A small, weathered temple to Manu still stands above the old village, quietly predating the resort town that grew up around it.

For most of its history Manali was a modest trading halt in the Kullu Valley, its economy built on apple orchards, sheep-rearing and caravans moving along the route toward Ladakh and Central Asia over the Rohtang Pass. It stayed largely off the map for outsiders until British officers and planters began exploring Kullu in the 1850s-60s, drawn by the cool climate, and planted the valley's first commercial apple orchards.

After 1947, and especially once the traditional trade route north closed in the 1960s, Manali reinvented itself as a hill resort — first for domestic honeymooners and families, then, through the 1970s, as a stop on the overland "hippie trail" that made Old Manali and nearby Vashisht known worldwide. Today it holds both identities at once: a mainstream Himalayan getaway and a slow-paced mountain-café town, while still serving as the last major halt before the high passes toward Leh and Spiti.

🧭 Tourist Information

Top Places to Visit in Manali

Snow Peaks, Solang Valley & Cafés — here's what most visitors build their itinerary around.

🏔️
Solang Valley
A wide glacial valley 14 km from town known for paragliding, zorbing, and skiing on its winter snow slopes.
🛕
Hadimba Devi Temple
A striking 16th-century wooden pagoda-style temple set inside a cedar forest, dedicated to a figure from the Mahabharata.
🏘️
Old Manali
A quieter village across the river with cafés, guesthouses and orchard walks — Manali's slower-paced side.
❄️
Rohtang Pass
A high mountain pass (permit and weather permitting) with snowfields even in early summer, about 3 hours from town.
♨️
Vashisht Hot Springs
Natural sulphur hot springs beside a centuries-old stone temple, a short drive from central Manali.
🌲
Manu Temple & Old Bazaar
The valley's namesake temple and Manali's original village core, predating the newer Mall Road strip.

Best Time to Visit Manali

Best Time
Mar – Jun
Pleasant days, blooming valleys and clear mountain views — the most comfortable window for sightseeing and road trips before monsoon.
Snow Season
Dec – Feb
Sub-zero nights but reliable snowfall around Solang and Old Manali; the pick for snow activities and winter photography.
Plan Carefully
Jul – Sep
Monsoon brings landslide risk on hill roads; travel is still doable with flexible timing and an early start.
🚕 Getting There

How to Reach Manali

🚗
By Road
Manali sits on NH3, about 570 km / 12–13 hrs from Delhi via Chandigarh — most travellers arrive by cab or overnight coach on this stretch.
🚆
By Rail
The nearest broad-gauge railhead is Chandigarh (~310 km); Joginder Nagar's narrow-gauge line is closer but considerably slower.
✈️
By Air
Bhuntar (Kullu-Manali) Airport is about 50 km away, with limited seasonal flights from Delhi and Chandigarh.

💡 Local Tips

Good to Know Before You Go

🧥
Pack layers — even summer evenings drop below 10°C once the sun sets.
🚗
Book your cab a day ahead in peak season (May–Jun, Dec–Jan); Manali-bound seats sell out fast.
🫁
If continuing to Rohtang or Leh, give yourself a day to acclimatise before gaining more altitude.
🍎
Try Himachali dham (a traditional festive thali) and fresh orchard produce from local stalls.
❓ FAQ

Manali Travel — Frequently Asked Questions

How far is Manali from Delhi by road?
+

Manali is about 570 km from Delhi — roughly 12–13 hours by cab via Chandigarh and the NH3 highway, depending on traffic and mountain-road conditions.

What is the best time to visit Manali?
+

March to June for pleasant weather and clear views, or December to February for snow. It's best to avoid the heaviest monsoon weeks (July–August) when hill roads can see landslides.

Is Rohtang Pass always open to visitors?
+

No — it's snowbound for much of winter and needs an entry permit in peak season, so it's worth checking current status before planning a same-day trip.

Can I book a one-way cab from Delhi to Manali?
+

Yes — One-Way Bhaarat runs flat-fare one-way cabs on the Delhi–Manali route, so you only pay for your own journey, not the driver's return leg.

How many days are enough to see Manali?
+

3–5 days comfortably covers Old Manali, Solang Valley, Hadimba Temple and a day trip toward Rohtang or nearby Kasol.
